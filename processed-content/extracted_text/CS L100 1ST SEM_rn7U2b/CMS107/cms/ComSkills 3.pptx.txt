   What is a formal/ official letter?            3/24
 - A formal letter is sometimes referred to as an official letter or a business 
     letter.
 -  It is a form of a letter which can be written from an individual to an 
    official, firm or an institution.
It can also be a letter written from an official, firm to an individual.
It can also be regarded as a letter written by an individual ( a subordinate) to 
    another individual(a superior). 
Letters of application for employment can be considered as formal.

Purposes for writing formal letters             5/24
Several reasons may account for writing such letters:
      i. You can write to the Registrar of a university to ask for a change of name in 
           case your name was wrongly spelt on your admission letter.
     ii. You can also write to a Head of Department to:
         a. seek clarification on courses offered in the Department.
         b. provide you with an introductory letter.  
      iii. You can equally write to the Dean to ask for permission to use a facility 
            under his/her care to organize a programme, etc. 
      iv. You can write and make a request for your transcript to be sent to an 
             institution you have applied for a job.

  The structure of a formal letter               7/24
A formal letter should have the following structure:
   i. Provide two addresses:
     a.  The writer’s address should be written on the top right 
            side of your paper.
                - The name of the writer SHOULD NOT APPEAR as part of the  
                   address. It is just the writer’s address and a date.
                - Put your date immediately after the address.
    b. The recipient’s/addressee’s address should be written on 
       the left side of the paper. There should however, be a space left after the 
       writer’s address in order to make it appear like a slant line between 
       the two addresses on your paper.
               - DO NOT ADD A DATE to the recipient’s address.

8

--- SLIDE 9 ---
     The structure of a formal letter                  8/24
 -ii. Provide an impersonal salutation:
       This is the opening of the letter and so, the writer needs to address   
        the recipient by using any of the following titles:
                -Dear Sir, Dear Madam,
                - Do not use a Dear Sir/Madam when you know the sex of the 
                  recipient.
                   
  iii. Provide a title/heading for the letter:
       -  People who occupy high positions receive quite a number of 
          letters in a day. It will, therefore, be prudent to provide a title for 
          your letter so that the recipient can  quickly get the content of the 
           letter.
      - The title/heading of your letter should be a summary of  your purpose for 
          writing.

--- SLIDE 10 ---
The formal letter, just like any form of essay, also has three parts:
The Introductory paragraph: It sets the tone for the letter. It contains the Thesis Statement which states the purpose for the formal letter
The Body paragraphs: These paragraphs develop the Thesis statement
The Concluding paragraph: It provides a summary of what has been discussed.

--- SLIDE 11 ---
     The structure of a formal letter                    9/24
iii. The body of the letter:
         -  This is where the writer provides in clear sentences the  
                 reason(s) for writing the letter. 
         - It is the content of the letter and since it is a formal letter, the 
              writer cannot ask of the condition of health of the recipient. 
            
        -  The writer needs to go straight to his/her reason(s) for writing.

11

--- SLIDE 12 ---
      The structure of a formal letter        10/24
- The reason(s) for writing should be developed in paragraphs.
    - Each sentence in the paragraph should have a clear meaning and 
         be very brief.
     -  Contracted forms such as can’t, I’m, cant, don’t, won’t, etc. should be avoided.
     - Thus, use I am, do not, cannot, will not, etc.
Provide a subscription after the body paragraph(s) are developed:
       - There is the need for the writer to disclose his/her identity by 
         introducing any one of the following:
        - Yours faithfully,
         - Yours sincerely,
         - Yours truly,

12

--- SLIDE 13 ---
The structure of a formal letter                    11/24
  -  Always ensure that the Y of Yours is a capital letter and the f of 
       faithfully is in a lower case alphabet. In the same way, ensure that the s of 
       sincerely and t of truly are in lower case alphabets respectively.  
   -  Don’t forget to put a comma after faithfully, sincerely or truly if you 
       punctuated the two addresses; if not, don’t punctuate the 
       subscription as well.
   v. Provide your signature before you add your name:
       - It is assumed that your signature cannot be read and so, 
          when you add your name, you are interpreting the signature for 
          your recipient to know the name of the writer. 
       - It will be unacceptable and inappropriate to provide your name before the 
          signature.

       Language of a formal Letter                   12/24
Formal letters are mostly written to recipients who do not share close 
    affinity with the writers of such letters.
There is a social distance between the writer and recipients. 
 This distance relationship, calls for a careful selection of words. 
The writer, in most cases, does not know the recipient and so, the language should be formal.
 If the language is formal, it means that the choice of words for communication should be appropriate, polite and acceptable. 
 Language which is appropriate should  avoid the use of slangs,  
    colloquialism and contracted forms.  
Language which is polite should avoid insinuating expressions, 
    threatening words and provocative remarks.

DISCUSSING THE FEATURES OF A FORMAL LETTER                                                                                                                                          16/24 
Let us examine the features of a formal letter in the letter on slides 15 and 16. 
 1. In the first place, you can identify two addresses: 
                   a) the writer’s address and the date, and 
                   b) the recipient’s address.
2. Also,  you can find that the writer’s address does not begin with the writer’s 
             name.
3. The date is placed after the writer’s address and not the recipient’s 
      address.
4. There is a salutation after the recipient’s address. 
5. The salutation does not mention Dear Sir/ Madam. It is very important 
     that when you know the sex of the addressee, you need not address 
    him/her as Dear Sir/ Madam. Use a single sex in most cases. In the   
    salutation above, you know that the Registrar of University of Cape Coast is a male, so, 
    you need to use Dear Sir, NOT Dear Sir/ Madam. 

18

--- SLIDE 19 ---
DISCUSSING THE FEATURES OF A FORMAL LETTER                                                                                   17/24
6. Note that the next item after the salutation is the title/ heading.
     - The title should be brief and straight on point. There is no need to 
        provide a long  and winding title.
     - Do not put a full stop after the title because the title you provide is 
       not a sentence or a statement. The title is a phrase and a phrase 
       does not take a full stop.

7. The next item is in connection with the introduction.
     - Provide a brief introduction about yourself for your recipient to know 
       who you are, instead of wasting the time to ask of the condition of health 
       of the recipient. That information will be irrelevant.

8.  The body of the letter is the next item to consider. 
        - State the reason(s) for writing.
        - If it is a piece of information that you need, go ahead and state it.
  9.  The conclusion follows the body of the letter.
       - Note that this type of conclusion is not used to summarize the content 
          of the letter. 
      -  Use this opportunity to acknowledge the ability of the recipient to help  
          you get what you need. 
      - In the example given, you can find that such an acknowledgement is  
          stated to demonstrate your trust in the recipient and to thank him/her .

       - You ensure that you subscribe by using an impersonal
