MINUTES WRITING
Have you had the opportunity to write the minutes of a meeting?
How did you find it?
Did you write everything that was said at the meeting?
Why do you think writing minutes is important?
I hope by the end of this session, you will be able to write good minutes for any meeting.

--- SLIDE 3 ---
Minutes constitute a record of decisions made at a meeting
Minutes writing is done objectively 
It is the final decisions made at a meeting that are recorded in minutes




--- SLIDE 4 ---
TYPES OF MINUTES
Narrative Minutes: These are detailed type of minutes. They entail resolutions made and actions to be taken.
Resolution Minutes: These are not as detailed as narrative minutes. They contain only the key decisions made at the meeting.
Action Minutes: These are also not as detailed as narrative minutes. They present the actions to be taken on decisions made at the meeting.                     

--- SLIDE 5 ---
LANGUAGE OF MINUTES
Reported speech: Minutes are recorded in reported speech style. The writer reports what members say.
Third person point of view: Minutes are written about some people or things. Minutes are about  the pronouns he, she, it and they. Minutes are recorded objectively. They are not about the writer.
Tense: Minutes are normally recorded in the past tense. It is incumbent upon the writer to maintain the appropriate sequence of tense. 
   

--- SLIDE 6 ---
STUCTURE OF MINUTES
1. Title
Name of Association/Organization
Date
Time
Place
Eg.: Minutes of the meeting of the Association of Students of Communication Studies Held at Auditorium 900 on 4th April 2020 


--- SLIDE 7 ---
STRUCTURE OF MINUTES, CONT.
2. Attendance (Roll Call)
3. Opening
4.Reading of previous minutes
5. Acceptance of minutes
It is a member who was present at the previous meeting who moves for acceptance or otherwise
6. Matters arising from previous minutes
7. Main or new business
8. Other matters or Any other business(AOB)
9. Closure(A member moves for closure)
10. Endorsement


--- SLIDE 8 ---
IMPORTANCE OF MINUTES
Minutes serve as a reflection of consensus
They act as a point of reference for the discussions and decisions that transpired at a meeting
They serve as a guide
They are used for evaluation

--- SLIDE 11 ---
OPENING 
The house commenced proceedings at 4:30pm. The President called the house to order and he welcomed members in a special way.
 
Reading of Previous Minutes
The Vice-President led the house through the minutes of the previous meeting and after some corrections were effected, Prof. Anamuah-Mensah moved for their acceptance and he was seconded by Ms. Ivy Akakpo.


--- SLIDE 12 ---
MATTERS ARISING FROM THE PREVIOUS MINUTES
Environmental Committee
At an earlier meeting, the environmental committee was to furnish the house with a strategic plan of activities. However, the committee did not report on the environmental concerns of the Association since their chairman was absent.
Street Naming
Mr. Bonney, the surveyor, had assured the Association, at an earlier meeting, that he had procured the master plan. He promised to make it available to ease the Association’s own input in the matter of the street naming activity. The President had been working on the procurement of the master plan. However, he had not received it yet. The President had also hinted the house on the cost implication of the procurement of the master plan.


--- SLIDE 13 ---
Police Patrol
The treasurer had paid all the arrears of allowances due the Police patrol team from December, 2019. However, it has been observed that the Police patrol team had not been regular and effective in the discharge of their duties in the community. The house therefore concluded that the Police showed up when they had received their allowances. 
The President and Vice-President would meet with Mr Govina to discuss the mode of Police patrol in the community.         
Change of signatories   
The Secretary was to write a letter through Mr. Dennis to the manager of Zenith Bank for the change of signatories to the Association’s bank accounts.  
 


--- SLIDE 14 ---
Security Issues
The house talked about security consciousness. The activities of tricycle drivers and scrap dealers in the community should be viewed with suspicion. The President and Mr Dennis commended members who checked on them when they suspected a strange sound and an unfamiliar parked vehicle.
New Members
In addition to letters being written to invite new members to join the Association, it was suggested that the Executive made personal calls on new residents to introduce the Association to them and to invite them, in a persuasive way, to our meetings.
 


--- SLIDE 15 ---
ANY OTHER BUSINESS
 
End-of-year Get-together
The end-of-year get-together was on the 26th of January 2020 and it was organized by the Welfare Committee. The event was preceded by a brief meeting by the house. Members contributed GHC20 per person towards it. The house was refreshed by the Grace Hill School of Technology. The children present were entertained and the ambience was blissful. 
 
Zoomlion
The Treasurer has assumed the money collection duties for Zoomlion. All payments should be channeled through him. This will streamline communication between the community and Zoomlion. 
 
Police Post
Mr. Dennis gave an update on the police post. The Association should make the necessary contacts in order to get hold of the site plan.
Security Systems
The house was briefed by Prof. Anamuah-Mensah on the Ring.Com and Wazy security systems. According to Prof., the systems are online and interested members should be connected to the Internet. The security systems provide alerts and recordings on occurrences in the house. A user could retrieve information on previous activities in the house. The cost of a single set of the Ring.com device is $150 while the Wazy is $20.
    

 


--- SLIDE 16 ---
CLOSING
In the absence of any further deliberations, Prof. Anamuah-Mensah moved for the closure of the meeting. He was seconded by Prof. Akua Britwum. The meeting finally ended at 5:25pm.
