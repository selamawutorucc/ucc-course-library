What is a Report?
A report is an informed account of a situation which helps audience to know and take informed decisions.
 A report is a description of an event, an experience, situation or action which is meant to give a reliable and accurate piece of information about its subjects or the event it covers.
The presentation of report consists of three main activities namely: Researching (looking for facts); Analysis and interpretation of information; and Presentation of analysed facts.


--- SLIDE 4 ---
What is Report Writing?
 Report writing is an account of a sistuation based on analysed facts. It includes conclusions and suggestions drawn from the analysed data.
Report writing takes cognizance of particular readership, particular situations and particular relationships between a reader and a writer.

Types of Reports
 Reports can be classified into types based on subject matter, form or length. 
 Subject matter reports include: Eye witness report, Work report, Progress report, Investigative report, Laboratory report etc.
 Report types based on form include: Schematic form, Letter form, Memorandum form and Mixed form
 Report types based on length include: Long, Medium and Short report.


--- SLIDE 7 ---
Types of Report Based on Form
Schematic Form: Ideas presented in a report can be arranged into sections and subsections with appropriate headings and subheadings. The schematic form of a report breaks ideas down for the reader and makes ideas more readable. 
Letter Form: usually adopts the structure of an official letter.  This is suitable when the subject matter being presented is a simple one with a single point of view. 
Mixed Form: combines features of the letter form and feature of the schematic form. It begins like a letter and takes the schematic form when the findings are written down.

--- SLIDE 8 ---
Types of Report Based on Subject Matter 
 Eye-witness Report: gives an account of any event or experience on issues suh as accidents, thefts, labour unrest, armed robbery, squabbles. This type of report is normally presented in a narrative form and chronological order.
Work Report: presents information on tasks confronting an establishment or a parastatal. Thus the information provided must be on job related tasks.
Laboratory Report: This gives an account of the procedure, result and significance of an experiment with findings and conclusions. It outlines, analyses and, evaluates facts scientifically to form a report.

--- SLIDE 9 ---
Types of Report Based on Length
Long reports are detailed account of an event or situation usually with the prefatory and appendage parts.  
Short reports are less detailed usually they do not contain prefatory nor appendage parts.
Here, the length depends on the degree of information required.

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Structure of Report 
 Introduction: serves to prepare the mind of the reader for the findings and conclusions. It may include parts such as  definition of terms, collection of data, mode of analysis and interpretation and layout of report.
Main Body: presents the collected data in relation to the problem. The body of effective reports is generally demarcated into parts.
 Conclusion: communicates summary, conclusions or recommendations of the reports. 


--- SLIDE 11 ---
Additional Parts of Reports
Prefatory parts of a reports are formal documents and topics which serve to identify the report. They include: 
Title fly: states just the title of the report.
 Title page: comprises the title of the report, names of the investigator and names of the authorizer. 
Letter of submission: is a document which formally transmits the report to the recipient.



--- SLIDE 12 ---
Prefatory Parts of Reports
Letter of authorization: is the document that authorized the investigator to write the report. 
Table of content: provides a list of the various aspects of the report and their corresponding pages.
Executive summary or sysnopsis: recaps the main points,  conclusions and suggestions.

--- SLIDE 13 ---
Appendage Parts of Reports
 Appendage are usually supplementary documents or references attached to the report. It comprise Appendix and Bibliography.
Appendix contains documents which will help the reader to understand the nature of the investigation.
Bibliography features generally when investigation involves certain theoretical positions. They are alphabetical list of all books, journals, articles and newspapers consulted by the investigator.

Features of a Good Report
 Accuracy: a good report must be accurate and factual. This will prove that the report is authentic and well treated.
Clarity: a good report must be clear or lucid. The facts presented in the report must not be vague or confusing. The writer should select apt words suitable for his/her subject matter. The information contained in a report should be direct and straight forward and should be able to capture the message intended.




--- SLIDE 16 ---
Features of a Good Report
Logicality and Objectivity: Facts in report writing must be logical and objective without any room for possible misinterpretation, ambiguities and vagueness. The writer must avoid any form of sentimentality, subjectivity, prejudice and the like. 
Coherence: A good report must be coherent. The structure must be perfectly adapted to its subjects and be presented step by step and logically too.
Formality: A good report should be formal. Thus, formal writing should be employed. This simply means writing in full and avoiding slang or colloquialisms and using words correctly.


--- SLIDE 17 ---
Features of a Good Report
Brevity: a good report must not be too long. It must be stratight forward devoid of verbosity. However, some reports are long because they contain a great deal of important information and the information may be required as in the case of a detailed report.
A good report must be based on well researched and analysed data. It is focused on the problem and need at stake, and helps in resolving them. It is presented in a recommended style.



--- SLIDE 18 ---
Importance/Uses of a Report 
Since reports basically communicate pieces of information obtained through thorough investigation and research, it is a reservoir of reliable information which serves humanity in diverse forms and contributes to the growth and development of the society.
Report provides accurate information on diverse subject matters. Information provided in a report helps institutions, organizations and corporate bodies in planning and effective management. 
Report is useful in equipping any management with correct choice and effective decision making which on the long run translates to effective management and operational competence.


--- SLIDE 19 ---
Importance/Uses of Report
Reports also serve as effective means of communication within organizations. They provide vehicle for the cross flow of information, from management to employees and then feedback from employees to management.
 Reports help in solving current organizational and societal problems. Reports do not end without recommendations and guidelines which when applied deals with complicated problems.
