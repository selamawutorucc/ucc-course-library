TOPIC SENTENCES 
 
Usually the first sentence of a paragraph in the body of an essay is a topic sentence. 
The topic sentence tells the purpose of the paragraph and connects the paragraph to the thesis. 
The topic sentence has three parts: 
 A transition indicates the idea in the paragraph is one of several supporting the thesis. 
 A reminder of the thesis connects the paragraph to the purpose of the paper. 
 The main idea of the sentence tells the main idea of the paragraph. 
 
WRITING THE TOPIC SENTENCE 
1. 
Think before you begin. Ask yourself: What do I want to discuss in the paragraph? 
2. 
Before you begin the paragraph, jot down some ideas and develop a list of what you want to 
discuss in the paragraph. Look over the list and determine which ideas belong together, and 
which should be saved for another paragraph. Then plan a topic sentence that is suitable for 
the list you have developed. 
3. 
Check that your topic sentence clearly covers the specifics you will discuss in your 
paragraph. 
4. 
As you are writing your paragraph, and after you have finished it, check your topic sentence 
again:  
 Is it focused on one main idea?  
 Does it clearly state what you intend to discuss?  
 Has your paragraph been properly limited and controlled by your topic sentence?  
 If you see a problem with what you have written, revise your topic 
sentence or revise your paragraph, which ever seems better. 
5. 
Check that your topic sentence has all three parts: 
 a transition, 
 a reminder of the thesis, 
 the main idea – the purpose of the paragraph.

Dr. Rodney 
 
 
When a person thinks of that old-time, small-town doctor he usually 
envisions a mannerly, dignified gentleman. However this image did not fit 
my Uncle Rodney, a doctor in the small town of Bandon, Wyoming. Instead, 
Doctor Rodney was an obnoxious person because he had an annoying habit 
of speaking in crude, incoherent sentences; he had sloppy eating habits; and 
he was a messy smoker.  
 
Probably Doctor Rodney's most irritating trait was his crude way of 
speaking. For example, I recall a particularly embarrassing moment during a 
family reunion at my mother-in-law's house when Dr Rodney was asked to 
say a blessing before dinner. He managed a “Hump, bump, grump," or so it 
sounded, and almost immediately added "Goddammit" as he knocked over a 
bowl of grated corn he was grabbing. As a result, my mother-in-law – a very 
religious person – was mortified. On another occasion, Dr. Rodney's nurse 
said, "It's a good thing I can interpret what Dr. Rodney says and smooth over 
the rough feelings, or we would be out of patients."  
 
Additionally, Doctor Rodney bothered many people with his messy 
eating habits. He shoveled food into his mouth at such an alarming rate that 
often he could not catch his breath. My brother- in-law once remarked, 
"When I see Uncle Rodney eat I think of jackals devouring their kill." 
Furthermore, Doctor Rodney always finished his meal long before anyone 
else; then he would make a nauseating slurping sound by sucking air and 
saliva through the gaps between his top front teeth while he waited for 
everybody else to finish. Because of his atrocious eating habits, none of 
Doctor Rodney's neighbors invited him to dinner.  
 
Doctor Rodney was also disliked because he was an inconsiderate 
smoker. Everywhere he went, he left a trail of ashes, a terrible stench, and 
wet, chewed-up cigar butts. After his death, the office 
cleaning lady confided that the townspeople used to 
bet on how many days would pass before anyone saw 
Dr. Rodney without a spot of tobacco juice in his shirt. 
Naturally, all the local children learned not to be 
downwind from him because no one could easily 
tolerate his odor of stale tobacco.  
 
Clearly, Dr. Rodney was an obnoxious person whose talking, eating, 
and smoking habits alienated him from even his own family. He was indeed 
lucky that the town had only one doctor, or he might not have been 
employed. 
Identifying the Parts of a Topic Sentence 
 
Thesis: “Instead, Doctor Rodney was an obnoxious person because he had an 
annoying habit of speaking in crude, incoherent sentences; he had sloppy 
eating habits; and he was a messy smoker.” 
  
The parts of the topic sentence in the second paragraph are: 
 
Transition:      
 
The word "most" tells the reader that the 
 
 
 
first of several reasons will be presented 
 
 
 
in this paragraph. 
 
Reminder of the Thesis:  The words, "irritating trait," repeat the 
 
 
 
idea of the thesis that Doctor Rodney is 
 
 
 
obnoxious. 
 
Main Idea of Paragraph:  The paragraph will focus on "his crude 
 
 
 
way of speaking." 
 
 The parts of the topic sentence in the third paragraph are: 
 
Transition:      
 
"Additionally" suggests that this  
 
 
 
paragraph will discuss another reason 
 
 
 
that supports the thesis. 
 
Reminder of the Thesis: 
 "Doctor Rodney bothered many people" 
 
 
 
connects to the thesis word "obnoxious." 
 
Main Idea of Paragraph:  "his messy eating habits" tells the reader 
 
 
 
the subject of this paragraph. 
 
The parts of the topic sentence in the fourth paragraph are: 
 
Transition:      
 
 "Also" indicates that the third supporting 
 
 
 
idea will be explained in this paragraph. 
 
Reminder of the Thesis:  "was also disliked" repeats the idea of the 
 
 
 
thesis. 
 
Main Idea of Paragraph:  This paragraph is about how Doctor 
 
 
 
Rodney is an "inconsiderate smoker."
