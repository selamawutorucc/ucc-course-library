Introduction
A letter is one person's written message to another pertaining to some matter of common concern. Letters can serve several purposes: when intimates are forced to spend time at great distances from one another, letters allow them to maintain the relationship; rulers, scholars, merchants, officials, and professionals use them to conduct affairs with their far-flung correspondents; poets and other writers may also use them as vehicles for self-expression. Letters contribute to the protection and conservation of literacy.
There are three main types of letters and they include;
Informal letters
Semi-formal letters
Formal letters
But we will lay more emphasis on the formal letters.

Formal Letters
Formal letters are one of the most common form of official letters, framed and presented in almost all organizations, be it companies, schools, colleges, business firms, institutions, agencies, or any departments of corporate sectors. In general, what you put together will have one of the following purposes:

Features of Formal Letter
Addresses:
An address is one of the compulsory components of a letter. Regardless of what type of letter you are writing, you simply cannot write a letter without putting an address. A formal or an official letter will require that the writer supply two addresses that is;
Writer’s AddressThe return address should be written in the top right-hand corner of the letter. That indicates the address of the person writing the letter.
Recipient Address The inside address should be written on the left, starting below your date. It mostly indicates the address of the receiver.

Features of Formal Letter
Slant Method Address
The slant method of writing an address is very similar to an upturned triangle or pyramid. Here, the address is written in such a manner that it resembles an upside down pyramid or triangle; when the date of the address is added to it then it begins to look more like an hourglass.
Example of a slanted address is:
Obuatupam Nkatenkwam Primary School,
   P.O. Box 223,
     Kumasi.

Features of Formal Letter Cont.
Body
The body, contains paragraph that merge to elaborate on the heading sated above in your letter.  It has three main parts which are;
First /introductory Paragraph: The first paragraph of formal letters should include an introduction to the purpose of the letter. It's common to first thank someone or to introduce yourself and show the motive behind the letter.
Main Paragraphs: Normally, the following paragraphs should provide the main information of the letter, and build on the main purpose in the introductory or first paragraph. Mostly formal letter has main ranging between 2-5 paragraphs.
Final/concluding Paragraph: The final paragraph should shortly summarize the intent of the formal letter and end with some call to action. 
Note: “Thank you” is part of the concluding paragraph but, is written on the next line of the concluding paragraph.

Languages Of Formal Letters
Use proper spelling: Use correct spelling all of the time. This includes avoiding short forms and only using words that are found in the dictionary. This means that you must write "tonight" instead of "2nite" and "because" instead of "bc.“
Use proper punctuation: Question marks and to the ellipsis (which should have only three dots). In addition, you should also void the ampersand (&); write out the word "and.“
Use full words: Contractions are appropriate only for conversational use and for informal writing, never for technical or formal writing. Note that the full form of “can't" is one word: "cannot" not "can not". You should also avoid using abbreviated words such as "TV" and "photo" and use the full versions such as "television" and "photograph".
Do not write as you would speak: So, like, you know, I totally don't even do that OMG! To ensure you are maintaining a formal tone, you need to leave out words and phrases that are not essential to conveying your point. For example, avoid using "well," "like," "for sure," "you know," and similar phrases.

--- SLIDE 17 ---
Cont. of Languages  of Formal Letters
Emoticons should never be used: First of all, it is never acceptable to use emoticons, including smiley faces, sad faces, winks, etc. suggest finding another way to tell your boss you are very happy about your raise or you may be finding another job!
Avoid using first and second person pronouns: Formal writing often tries to be objective, and the pronouns "I" and "you" tend to imply subjectivity. Instead, use words such as "one," "one's," "the reader," the "author's," etc. For example:
Informal: "You should sleep eight hours each night."Formal: "One should sleep eight hours each night.”
Use transitional adverbs such as: 
"additionally" or "moreover"
"nevertheless" or "however"
"therefore" or "thus"
"alternatively" or "instead" or "otherwise"
