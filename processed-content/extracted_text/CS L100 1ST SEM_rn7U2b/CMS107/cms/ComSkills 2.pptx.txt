              INTRODUCTION
Whenever we speak or write, we engage in discourse. 

A discourse is simply the use of language for achieving a specific objective.

Good writers, therefore, write to express their informed opinions to their audiences.

--- SLIDE 3 ---
In order to write an informed opinion about a subject matter, one needs to write persuasively.

This means the essay should contain a purposeful message to convey to your readers.

One basic way to do so is to carefully weigh one’s own point of view before embarking on the writing process.

--- SLIDE 5 ---
THE EXPOSITORY ESSAY
This is the basic type of writing required in academic writing.
To write an expository essay requires that the writer has a well informed point of view or idea.
This idea is said to be the author’s thesis.
This means that basic research skills such as planning and organizing are key.
An expository essay usually exposes a writer’s idea to the reader, and thus requires a great deal of logic and structure in order to sound convincing.

--- SLIDE 7 ---
Writing the Introductory Paragraph
An effective introductory paragraph usually hooks the reader, and thus stimulates them to read the write-up. This can be achieved by following three basic guidelines:
1. Begin with an ice-breaker, using strategies such an anecdote, a historical antecedent, statistics/figures, facts, proverbs/wise sayings, or seldom explanation or definition. This should be done briefly though.
2. Next, state your thesis. It must be short, simple, and concise.
There are two basic types:
The personal thesis statement: e.g. In this essay, I explore three effects of the Covid 19 on Ghana’s economy.
The impersonal thesis statement: This essay explores three effects of …
3. Organize your thesis.
Tell the reader how you’ve planned to discuss the issues raised in the thesis in clear independent sentences.


--- SLIDE 8 ---
Trial exercise
Before you proceed with the lesson, write an introductory paragraph of between 100 and 120 words on the topic, Three Effects of Covid 19 on the Educational System of Ghana.

--- SLIDE 9 ---
Writing the body paragraphs
Here, concentrate on developing each idea stated in your thesis.
Each idea should be contained in a unique paragraph, beginning with a short and concise topic sentence.
For the sake of our discussion, your body paragraphs must be composed of three paragraphs only.
Conclude each body paragraph with a summary/concluding sentence.

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Writing the concluding paragraph
Don’t rush to get done with your essay.
On the contrary, see this section of the write-up as an opportunity to make lasting impression on your reader.
To this end, attempt to do the following:
Summarize the ideas in the essay.
Engage in advocacy.
Ask questions and make a follow up.
But do not introduce a new idea that has the potential to torpedo your own thesis.

Structure of the argumentative essay
The structure of this essay type is pretty much like that of the expository essay.
This time, however, it varies based on the number of points a writer can adduce in support of or against a major argument.
This will inform how the body of the essay is structured.
Use strategies discussed under the expository essay to develop issues in writing this type of essay.

--- SLIDE 16 ---
THE NARRATIVE ESSAY
In composing a narrative essay, a good writer aims to tell a compelling story that is believable.
The writer does so using all the language resources of the past tense and,  sometimes too the historic present.
But narrations of places, events, and times also go with the description of those places, events, and occasions.
For this reason, to narrate is to describe also.
A good mastery of colorful language such as adjectives, adverbs, and even verbs helps in the process.

--- SLIDE 17 ---
Structure of the narrative essay
There’s no one-size-fits all model.
The rule of thumb, however, is to organize the narration in a logical sequence, and marinate with loads of eye-opening adjectives and adverbs.
Try to be persuasive and truthful all the same.
You may also want to begin each paragraph with a clear topic sentence.

--- SLIDE 18 ---
Trial exercise
In not more than 250 words, write an essay on how you felt as a student, upon hearing about the closure of the University due to the outbreak of the Corona Virus pandemic in Ghana.

--- SLIDE 19 ---
THE DESCRIPTIVE ESSAY
This type of essay is designed for describing details of the subject. 
Here, the emphasis is on the use of sensory perception (e.g. visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory).
You are to describe the way it looks, smells or works. It can be compared with a detailed overview of the things you write about. In this type of essay, every detail counts.

--- SLIDE 20 ---
Generally, combine the ideas under the expository and narrative essays to compose beautiful descriptive essays.
How would you describe in 250 words, Ghana to one of your European friends who wants to come to Ghana on an exchange programme?

SUMMARY
In this discussion, we sought to understand what discourse types are.
We focused on four basic types and their uniqueness in academic writing.
We stressed that each discourse type is unique because it performs and achieves different communicative functions, one of which is to persuade the audience.
