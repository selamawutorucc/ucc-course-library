Curriculum Placement:
Entry-level, calculus-based, General Physics sequence
Pre-requisites:  None
Course Objectives:	The course covers the physical principles that underlie the field of classical mechanics. Introductory Mathematics will be incorporated to formulate quantitative statements. Heavy emphasis is placed on understanding the principles and applying them to solve “real” problems. The final grade is based principally on the student’s ability to recognize physical principles and apply mathematics to obtain solutions to quantitative problems.
Course Outline:	   
PHY 101: (Introductory Mechanics, Properties of Matter and Heat). 
Dimensional Analysis: Vectors, Kinematics; Dynamics, Work-Energy, Elasticity; FLUIDS-Pressure In fluid; Fluid flow; Viscosity; Surface tension; Kinetic Theory of Gases; Temperature Scales; First Law of Thermodynamics; Principal Molar Specific Heat Capacities of Gasses; Isothermal and Adiabatic Changes; Thermal Conductivity.
PHY 102: (Introductory Optics, Waves, Electricity & Magnetism)
Reflection & Refraction at Plane/Curved Surfaces, Lens’s Formula, Thin Lenses in Contact, 
Characteristics of Wave Motion, Sound Waves, Resonance, Static Electricity, The Coulomb’s Law, Electric Potential, Capacitance, Current Electricity
Methods of Evaluation:						
Homework: Homework(s) for each section of the course will be assigned. Homework is due one week after being assigned. Post-deadline homework will not be accepted. 
Exams: The student will complete five (5) tests (including Quizes) and a final exam. The exams consist of qualitative and quantitative problems similar to those discussed in class and those assigned for homework.
Laboratory: The student will complete different laboratory assignments. The laboratory assignments are in close relation with the basic concepts explained in the lectures. Each assignment involves carrying out an experiment and the completion of a brief laboratory report. 
Homework and examination grades collectively determine the final course grade according to the following formula:
Tests (homework, tests, including quizes): 		40 %
