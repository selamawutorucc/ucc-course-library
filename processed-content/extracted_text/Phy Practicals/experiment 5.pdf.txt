PROCEDURE 
The copper rod was connected to the mains with an ammeter as shown in the diagram above. The 
voltage source was set to 12V. The current was varied at constant intervals of 0.2A. The voltage 
drop across the rod at the two sockets on the side were measured and recorded using the 
amplifier for each current selected. The experiment was then repeated using the aluminium rod 
in place of the copper rod. A graph of voltage against current was plotted for each metal rod on 
the same graph sheet and the slope was determined. The slope was then used to determine the 
resistivity of the metal rods.

DISCUSSION 
The graph of voltage against current produces a slope which is equivalent to the resistance of the 
rod used i.e.           where R is the resistance of the rod. 
So for the copper rod the slope, and in that case the resistance, was found to be 1.207x10-5Ω. 
And for the aluminium rod, the slope or resistance was found to be 1.842x10-5Ω. The resistance 
calculated from both rods though are different from the theoretical values of resistances (  ) for 
copper and aluminium respectively. This deviation could probably be attributed to wrongful 
conversions and calculation.  
SOURCES OF ERROR 
Due to the use of the amplifier, there might have been wrong conversion of the values read into 
the actual values. 
PRECAUTIONS 
1. The experiment was repeated for each rod so as to produce consistent values on the 
voltmeter. 
2. Whenever the experiment was repeated, the power supply was switched off and the 
connection cords were inspected again to make sure they were all at their right places. 
CONCLUSION 
The resistance of the copper and aluminium rods was 1.207x10-5Ω and 1.842x10-5Ω respectively 
(a deviation from the theoretical values).
