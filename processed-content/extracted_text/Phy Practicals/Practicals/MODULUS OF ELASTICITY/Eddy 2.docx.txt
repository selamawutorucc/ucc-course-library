Theory: An elastic modulus or modulus of elasticity is the mathematical description of an object or substance’s tendency to be deformed elastically (that is non-permanently) when a force is applied to it. The elastic modulus of an object is defined as the slope of its stress-strain curve in the elastic deformation region. It describes the connection between tension and stretch with the deformation of a firm body with linear flexible behaviour. The modulus of elasticity is shortened with elastic module or as symbol with E. The majority of modulus of elasticity is module of elasticity.
Young’s modulus (E) describes tensile elasticity or the tendency of an object to deform along an axis when opposing forces are applied along that axis. It is often simply referred to as the Elastic Modulus. The value of the Young’s Modulus depends only on the material. The unit of the Young’s modulus of elasticity is Pa or N/ m2. Tensile strength is ratio of the magnitude of the external to the cross-sectional area. Tensile strength is defined as the ratio is defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length. A material having a large Young’s modulus is difficult to stretch and vice versa.
