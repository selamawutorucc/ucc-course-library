PROCEDURE
The experiment was set up as is shown in the diagram above. The viscometer was filled with the liquid glycerin. A temperature of 350C was maintained through the experiment using the thermostat and bath for about 15minutes. The alloy balls given were weighed on the electronic balance to determine their masses. The diameter of the ball was taken thereafter with the use of a micrometer screw gauge. Readings were obtained on each of the three ball bearings. These readings were recorded as d/mm. the ball bearings were dropped down the internal cylinder of the viscometer that is through the glycerin, one after the other. For each drop, the time taken for the ball to cover a specified distance was recorded using a stopwatch.

DISCUSSION 
   Viscosity refers to the degree of internal friction in a fluid. A highly viscous fluid is the one which possess high resistance on the motion of a body moving through it. Consider the equation proposed by Strokes, that is;
Viscosity of a liquid is directly proportional to the square of the radius but inversely proportional to the velocity of the sphere through it.
Hence from the experimental values obtained as the radius of the metal balls decreased the velocity in turn increased. It can therefore be deduced that as the size of the balls decreased the viscous drag on the balls also decreased and thus the smaller the size the smoother the travel of the ball in high velocity.
SOURCES OF ERRORS
Impurities in the glycerin may produce errors in reading.
Air bubbles may still be present in the glycerin.
Ball bearing may not be perfectly streamlined due to dents or scratches on them.
PRECAUTIONS
The metal ball was dropped gently to avoid the spilling of the glycerin.
Errors due to parallax were avoided during the reading of the distance on the inner cylinder.
CONCLUSION
Within all experimental limits, Stoke’s law was found to be 7.0586ms. Therefore as the radius of a sphere increases the viscous drag on the ball also increases and vice versa.
