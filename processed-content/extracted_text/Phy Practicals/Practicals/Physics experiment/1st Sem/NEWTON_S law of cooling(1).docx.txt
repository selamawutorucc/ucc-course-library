TITLE: EXPERIMENT TO VERIFY NEWTON’S LAW OF COOLING OF A LIQUID.
AIM   : To verify Newton’s law of cooling of a liquid – water
APPARATUS: Calorimeter with stirrer, thermometer (00-1000), gas burner, retort stand.
DIAGRAM:
Calorimeter with stirrer         Electric balance                    Glass beaker                Gas burner with retort stand
THEORY:
A hot liquid loses heat to the surrounding when it is left to cool over a period of time.  A graph of temperature against time of heat loss gives a curve known as cooling curve with a negative gradient.
  Newton’s law of cooling states that the rate at which heat is lost from a hot body as it cool down is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the hot body and the surrounding.

PROCEDURE:
A calorimeter was placed on a bad conductor and placed near open window. Room temperature is measured using a thermometer and recorded as θs0 0c. Water was heated in a beaker to 900c using the gas burner, retort stand. The calorimeter was filled with about more than half full of the heated water and covered with the thermometer and stirrer in place. Keeping the cooling liquid well stirred all the time the temperature of the liquid was recorded at 1minute (60 seconds) interval until the temperature dropped to 35 0c. The temperature of the surroundings was recorded after the experiment and recorded as θs10c. The mean room temperature was calculated for and recorded as θs00c. 
OBSERVATION:
Temperature of surrounding before experiment = 310c
Temperature of surrounding after experiment =290c
Average temperature of the surroundings= 300c
