TITLE: Determination of the water equivalent of a calorimeter.
AIM: To determine the water equivalent of a calorimeter.
APPARATUS: Copper calorimeter with outer jacket, stirrer made from bare copper wire, 0- 100 °C thermometer, electronic mass balance Libror EB-3200s-A , beaker and a stove. 
DIAGRAM
THEORY
Heat gained by calorimeter and cold water = Heat lost by warm water 
    M1Cw (θ3 – θ1) + McCc (θ3 – θ1) = MFCw (θ2 – θ3)
Where 
MI = Mass of calorimeter
MF = Mass of warm water
Cw = Specific heat capacity of water
Cc = Specific heat capacity of calorimeter
MICw (θ3 – θ1) + McCc (θ3 – θ1) = MFCw (θ2 – θ3)
But Cc = Cw since the water equivalent is the mass of water that has the specific capacity of the calorimeter 
Cw (MI + Mc) (θ3 – θ1) = MFCw (θ2 – θ3) 
(M1 + Mc) (θ3 - θ1) = MF (θ2 – θ3)
But MI = M1 – M0   MF = M2 – M1 and Mc = w
                     (M1- M0 + w)(θ3 – θ1) = (M2 -M1) (θ2 – θ3) 
METHOD
The calorimeter and stirrer were initially weighed together. Ice-cold water was then poured into the calorimeter until it was about one-third full, weighed on the Libror EB-3200s-A mass balance, and placed in its outer jacket and left to stand until its temperature was about 15℃ below the room temperature. Meanwhile, some water was heated up in a beaker until its temperature was about 15℃ above the room temperature. The stove was removed from underneath the beaker and after stirring both the warm water and the water in the calorimeter; their temperatures were taken separately and as accurately as possible. Some of the warm water was then poured from the beaker into the calorimeter until it was about two-thirds full. It was stirred and the highest temperature reached by the mixture about 5℃ above room temperature was recorded. The calorimeter with its contents and the stirrer was weighed again.

SOURCES OF ERROR
Errors due to the use of impure water.
Error may be due to heat transfer from hand to the compartment while holding the stirrer to stir. 
PRECAUTIONS
It was ensured that the mixture of warm water and the cold water in the calorimeter was well stirred to ensure uniform distribution of heat before the temperature was read.
It was ensured that the calorimeter was lagged (put in the outer jacket) to minimize the temperature loss in the water.
It was ensured that all temperatures were read accurately to obtain accurate results.
CONCLUSION
From the experiment, the water equivalent of a calorimeter is 2.5g
