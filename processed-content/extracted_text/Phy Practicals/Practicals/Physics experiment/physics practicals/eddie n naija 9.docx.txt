TITLE: DETERMINATION OF THE FOCAL LENGTH OF A CONVERGING LENS USING OPTICAL PINS.
AIM: To determine the focal length of a converging lens using the no parallax method.
APPARATUS: A convex lens, optical pins, meter rule, lens holder.
DIAGRAM
METHOD
An object pin O, a lens L, and a search pin S were placed in a straight line on an optical bench.  The pins were set in such a way that their tips lay approximately along the axis of the lens. The object pin O was placed at a distance (d) from the lens. The eye E was placed behind the search pin S. The search pin was moved towards or away from the convex lens until there was no parallax between the search pin and the image of the object pin. The distance between the object pin and the search pin (L) was measured and (I-d) was calculated. The experiment was repeated for other values of the object distance d and in each case the values for L-d, (L-d)/d were calculated. The results obtained were tabulated. A graph of (L-d)/d was plotted as ordinate against (L-d) as abscissa. The slope was calculated and the intercept determined from the graph. The reciprocal of the slope was then calculated.
THEORY
Lenses are optical instruments made from transparent materials that refract light. The distance between the focal point and the lens is the focal length, f. It is assumed that the lens is thin as compared to  such that it makes no difference whether  is measured between the focal point and either surface of the lens or the centre of the lens. A converging lens has a positive focal length.  The converging lens can form both real and virtual images depending on the position of the object from the lens.  When an object is located at a distance from the lens greater than twice the focal length, the image is small, real and inverted than the object. An object located a distance from the lens that is between the focal length and the twice the focal length, the image is real inverted and larger than the object. An object within f produces virtual, upright and larger image as compared to the object. The refractive power of a lens is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres.

DISCUSSION
The graph had a slope of 0.069cm-1 which represented  from the equation given. The reciprocal of the slope will gives the focal length (f) of the lens used and this was 14.49cm approximately. The focal length of the lens used was 14cm. Hence, the experimental value deviated by 0.49cm. This could be due to inaccurate calibration of the meter rule resulting in inaccurate readings. This consequently might have affected the focal length calculated from the graph and made it slightly different from the actual focal length of the lens. Random error during the experiment also affected the results. However, experiment can be concluded as being successful.
SOURCES OF ERROR
There were random errors during the experiment.
Scratches on the lens resulted in blurred images.
CONCLUSION
From the experiment conducted above, the focal length of the converging lens used in the experiment was obtained to be 14.49cm.
PRECAUTIONS
It was ensured that the lens was cleaned to enhance visibility.
The eye was placed exactly in front of the search pin and not at an angle above or below the pins.
A screen was placed behind the set up too ensure a clear view of the images formed.
The experiment was set up in a well lighted surrounding to produce clear images.
