TITLE:  Wheatstone Bridge Experiment
AIM: To determine the unknown resistance of a wire as a function of its cross-section.
APPARATUS: Resistance wire board, Slide wire measuring bridge, Connection box, Standard PEK carbon resistor, Unknown PEK carbon resistors, Power supply, 5V/1A, +/-15V, Digital multimeter 07134.00, Connection cord, 500mm, red, yellow andTHEORY: Wheatstone ridge is an instrument used in getting accurate resistance.For the Wheatstone bridge circuit, at the balanced point, the potential at C equals the potential at J and no current flows through the digital multimeter. The resistance, R of a metal in the form of a wire is directly proportional to its length l and inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area. That is
      and     R = ρ   ,
Where ρ is the constant of proportionality called Resistivity.
Resistivity is the resistance of a wire of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. The resistivity of a wire is also defined mathematically as;

METHOD: The experimental setup was as shown above.  Resister Q and the Standard resister R was connected using a connection box. The power supply was switched on and the correctness of the connection was checked by touching the bridge wire at the extreme ends (X and Y) with the slider (jockey) J and also the negative and positive readings were observed on the digital multimeter G. The balance point was located on the wire at J, the position where there is zero reading on the digital multimeter. The length l1 from the end X to the balanced point and also l2 was measured.  The experiment was repeated with the resistances wires of different diameters.
