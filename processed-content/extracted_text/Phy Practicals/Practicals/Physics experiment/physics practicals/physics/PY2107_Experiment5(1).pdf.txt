EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
1. Ensure that the unloaded triple beam balance reads zero. Weigh the Math-
iessen sinker in air. You should strive to obtain all mass measurements in this
experiment with accuracy to the smallest sub-division on the 0-1 gm scale, i.e.
a hundredth of a gram.
2. Heat the water to about 75◦C and very carefully transfer the beaker and water
to the support stand on the balance.
3. Carefully lower the bulb, suspended by a thread from the balance, into the
beaker of hot water. Ensure that the sinker is fully submerged. Weigh the bulb
again, ensuring that the beaker is not in contact with the balance, and record
the corresponding temperature of the water.
4. Record the temperature and the mass of the fully-submerged sinker as the
water cools to tap-water temperature. Initially, one reading should be taken for
every 0.1 gm mass decrease, which should correspond to two or three degrees
of temperature decrease. As the temperature becomes lower, you will notice a
steady growth in the temperature drop per 0.1 gm increase in the sinker mass.
When this temperature drop exceeds ﬁve degrees, reduce the mass increment
between readings to 0.05 gm.
Note: A convenient way to make the mass and temperature measurements is
as follows. After a measurement, adjust the rider on the 1-gm beam so that it is
sitting on the next mass increment you want to measure; initially, the sinker will
be somewhat more massive than the water it displaces (i.e. the beam pointer will
be above the balanced position). As the water cools, the beam pointer will come
down toward the balanced position - you can then wait for the beam pointer to
pass through the balanced position and read the temperature corresponding to
this time (you don’t have to scramble to read the mass, because you speciﬁed it
just after the previous measurement). Then adjust the rider on the 1-gm beam
so that it is sitting on the next mass you want to measure and carry on in this
fashion.
5. Initially, the cooling rate will be such that prompt recording of temperature
and mass will be required in order to achieve a good density of data points.
The cooling rate will gradually slow, since it is proportional to the constantly
decreasing diﬀerence between the temperatures of the water and air. The latter
stages of the cooling process can be accelerated by removing some water from
the beaker and replacing it with tap water using the equipment provided. But,
if you do this, be careful not to disturb the balance, and ensure that any drops
of water that attach to the balance or its wire frame are dried oﬀ!
