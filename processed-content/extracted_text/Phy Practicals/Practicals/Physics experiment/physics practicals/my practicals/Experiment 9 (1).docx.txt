TITLE: DETERMINATION OF THE FOCAL LENGTH OF A CONVERGING LENS USING OPTICAL PINS.
AIM: To determine the focal length of a converging lens using the no parallax method.
APPARATUS: A convex lens, optical pins, meter rule, lens holder.
DIAGRAM
METHOD
An object pin O, a lens L, and a search pin S were placed on an optical bench.  The pins were set in such a way that their tips lay approximately along the axis of the lens. The object pin O was placed at a distance (d) from the lens. The eye E was placed behind the search pin S. The search pin was moved towards or away from the convex lens until there was no parallax between the search pin and the image of the object pin. The distance between the object pin and the search pin (L) was measured and (I-d) was calculated. The experiment was repeated for other values of the object distance d and in each case the values for L-d, (L-d)/d were calculated. The results obtained were tabulated. A graph of (L-d)/d is plotted as ordinate against (L-d) as abscissa. The slope was calculated and the intercept determined from the graph. The reciprocal of the slope was found and its significance indicated.
THEORY
Light can travel through vacuum as well as other material such as glasses, air and water. The atoms of a material absorb, reemit or scatter the light. Therefore the speed of light in any material is slower than in vacuum and the speed of light depends on the nature of the material. Refraction is the change in the direction of light ray when it travels through different materials. The index of refraction of a material describes the extent to which the speed of the light in a medium differs from that of vacuum. Lenses are optical instruments made from transparent materials that refract light. They refract light such that the image of the source of light is formed. An object at infinity which is centred on the principal axis of a converging lens produces parallel rays to the principal axis which eventually converge at the focal point of the lens after passing through the lens. A convex lens is one whose width is small compared to the focal length of the lens. It is known as converging because it causes incident rays parallel to the principal axis to converge at the focal point. A converging lens has a curved surface with a thickened centre unlike a diverging lens. The distance between the focal point and the lens is the focal length, f. It is assumed that the lens is thin as compared to  such that it makes no difference whether  is measured between the focal point and either surface of the lens or the centre of the lens. A converging lens has a positive focal length.  The converging lens can form both real and virtual images depending on the position of the object from the lens.  When an object is located at a distance from the lens greater than twice the focal length, the image is small, real and inverted than the object. An object located a distance from the lens that is between the focal length and the twice the focal length, the image is real inverted and larger than the object. An object within f produces virtual, upright and larger image as compared to the object. The refractive power of a lens is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres.

SOURCES OF ERROR
Error caused may be due to parallax between the search pin and the image of the object pin.
Error caused may also be due to the scratches on the lens.
PRECAUTIONS

Error of parallax was avoided by moving the eye from left to right
It was ensured that the optical pins were set in a way that their tips lay approximately along the axis of the lens.
