AIM: To graphically show the relation of the time for vertical oscillation of a helical spring to the load and determine the effective mass of the spring (m) from the graph.

This experiment revolves around the Hooke’s law which states that the extension produced in a material is proportional to the applied load provided the elastic limit is not exceeded. Taking the force constant of the spring to be F (Newton per meter) and the load under the spring to be M (kg), if the spring is pulled X meters, the extra restoring force called into play is by the Hooke’s law equal to FX Newton’s. Procedure
A spring was suspended from a clamp on a firm support and a load by means of the slotted weight was attached to the lower free end of the spring until it was possible to measure the time for 20 vertical oscillation made by the load. Then the experiment was repeated for a second timing. This whole procedure was repeated with increasing values of the load. Each time, the time was measured for 20 vertical oscillations twice.

Discussion
The graph produced by the experimental values produces a straight line nearly passing through the origin. Again a negative intercept is also obtained with a positive gradient showing that the mass of a load is directly proportional to the time of vertical oscillation of a helical spring. Also the mass of the spring which is usually neglected is almost insignificant compared to the masses that were used in the experiment. This explains why the mass is not considered in theoretical equation.  
Sources of error
Errors in reading might have resulted from the air resistance in the room and wind passing in the room.
Errors might have resulted in the reading of time since it quite impossible to exactly measure the time of release to the end of the 20th oscillation.
Precaution
Windows and doors were closed at the time of the experiment to prevent wind from rushing into the room.
The load on the spring was displaced by a small distance 
Conclusion
Hence it can be conclude that the time of oscillation of a helical spring depends on the load mass and also that the mass of the helical spring is relatively small.
