THEORY
The Wheatstone bridge gives a precise method to measure resistance against a known standard.  Within a Wheatstone bridge, a comparative device measures two additional relative resistances from two separate resistors. The Wheatstone bridge utilizes repetitive comparisons of potentials to find the equipotential settings.  Within this experiment, a multimeter is used as the null detector and is placed as shown in the diagram.  When current flows through the multimeter and the jockey is suitably adjusted the current flowing through the multimeter becomes zero. Under these conditions the Wheatstone bridge is said to be balanced or there is “null deflection”. The potential difference across R equals that across the length, l2 and the potential difference across Rx equals l1 .

METHOD
The apparatus was set as shown in the diagram. The resistances to be investigated are shown as Rx. Resistor, Q and the Standard resistor, R were connected using the connection box. The power supply was switched on and the connection was checked for correctness by touching the bridge wire at the extreme ends (X and Y) with the slider (jockey), J and observing negative and positive readings of the digital multimeter, G. The balanced point was located on the wire at J, the position where there was zero reading on the digital multimeter. The lengths l1 from the end X to the balanced point and also l2 were measured. The measuring instrument G was balanced to zero by moving the slider was balanced to zero. The experiment was repeated with resistance wire of different diameters.  The resistances of Q and each of the wires was calculated and for each wire the cross-sectional area A was calculated. The results were tabulated.
