TITLE   An experiment to investigate the ’s law of cooling for a liquid.
AIM    To verify Newton’s law of cooling for water.
APPARATUS
Copper calorimeter with outer jacket, copper stirrer, thermometer (0C - 100C), beaker, ice, analytical balance (±0.01g), electric heater
DIAGRAM
THEORY
Newton’s law of cooling states that for a small difference of temperature between a body and its     surroundings, the rate of gain or loss of heat by the body is proportional to the difference in temperature between the body and the surroundings.

METHOD
A calorimeter is placed on a wooden board (bad conductor) and placed near an open window. Room temperature is measured using a thermometer and recorded as 1C. Water is heated in a beaker to 90C, using an electric heater. The calorimeter is filled with the heated water to within a centimeter from the top and the lid is put on top. The thermometer and the stirrer are put in place. Keeping the cooling water well stirred, the temperatures at one minute intervals were taken till it drop to 35C. The room temperature was recorded again at the end of the experiment with a thermometer and was recorded as 2C. The results were recorded in a tabular form. The mean room temperature was obtained by finding the average of 1C and 2C.

DISCUSSION
From the graph, it was observed that from the 1st minute to the seventh minute, the temperature was reducing by 2 and then from the 8th minute   onwards, the temperature reduced by every 1 with some of the values repeating themselves. Thus the nature of the curve was in a descending form with negative slopes. From the table and graph showing the mean temperature against the slope it is seen that the graph gives a straight line indicating that the rate of loss of heat is proportional to the excess temperature at any instant.
SOURCES OF ERROR
1. The handling of the thermometer constantly supplied heat to it and so affected the accuracy reading.
2. The holes in the calorimeter lid were quite big for the thermometer and so affected results since heat could be lost through the holes. 
PRECAUTION
1.  To ensure uniform distribution of heat and rapid cooling of the water the mixture was stirred carefully and continuously.
2. It was ensured that the calorimeter was lagged and covered to prevent heat loss to the surroundings.
3. It was ensured that the water boiled to the required temperature (900C) and then transferred quickly into the calorimeter to prevent heat loss.
CONCLUSION
. From the graph of change in temperature against the slope, it can be                                            concluded   that  α (θ-θο ) verifies Newton’s law since it gives a straight    line.
