THEORY

Loads greater than the elastic limit must not be added to the helical spring otherwise the spring becomes permanently strained and is then useless. METHOD
A spiral spring was suspended from a firm support. A mass of 70g was affixed to the free end of the spring, which caused a small depression. The spring together with the mass was set through a vertical vibration by exciting it through a small displacement. The periodic time, T, for 20 oscillations was then measured with a stop watch. The period for five other values of mass, M, in steps of 20g (i.e. M=90g, 110g, 130g and 150g) was taken. In each case, two timings for 20 oscillations were taken. The experiment was repeated then three more times. The results were recorded and tabulated.

 CALCULATIONS
From the graph the y-intercept is 9.71.Hence the mass of the spring is 9.71g.
DISCUSSION
The aim of this experiment is to determine the mass of the spring used. To get the mass of the spring, the spring was clamped to fixed support; the spring loaded with load and excited through a small displacement. The time for complete 20 oscillations was then recorded. When the loads are added to the spring, they cause the spring to be under tension due to the force of gravity acting on the loads. This then stretches the spring until equilibrium is reached. When the loads are moved away from the equilibrium by a distance A and then released, a net force acts towards the equilibrium position. This net force causes the loads to return to their equilibrium position. As the loads return to their equilibrium position, the net force decreases and the speed with which the system moves increases. The system continues to move until it reaches the maximum position equal to  on each side of the equilibrium. This to and fro movement is termed oscillation. This motion stops at A=0(equilibrium position).From the experiment it was realized that increase in loads increased the period. The actual mass of the spring is 9.4g but the experimental value is 9.71.The difference 0.11g is due to errors committed in the experiment. Air resistances influenced the values, apparatus error also inclusive.
SOURCES OF ERROR
(1)Air resistance from opened windows in the laboratory affected the oscillation.
(2)Buoyancy effect on the balance affected the real mass of the spring to be altered
PRECAUTION
(1)All the fans in the room were turned off.
(2)It was made sure that the oscillated vertically and not horizontally.
(3)The spring was displaced through small amplitude.
CONCLUSION
The mass of the helical spring is 9.71g.
