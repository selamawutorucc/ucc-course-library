PROCEDURE 
The apparatus was set up as shown in the diagram above. The string was laid across 2 triangular 
sliders and clamped between a fixed hook and a spring balance as shown in the diagram. A 
spring balance was attached to the string tensioner with a fishing line. The tensioning force was 
set to no greater than 30-40N or otherwise the string would have got broken. The triangular 
sliders along the measuring scale were used to adjust the string length. The vibrating of the string 
was optically scanned midway through the sliders. The signal from the photo cell, fitted with an 
aperture slit was amplified and transmitted to the oscilloscope and the counter. 
The string was vibrating by a gentle tap with the rubber hammer. The counter only started when 
the vibration had died away. 
The tension in the string was recorded as well as the diameter of the nickel wire. With the 
tension constant at the recorded value, a string length of 40cm was measured between the two 
sliders. The counter device was set to frequency (kHz). The sensor was aligned to the string and 
light so that the counter would read zero. 
The START button was pressed and the wire was gently tapped with the rubber hammer to start 
vibration. The STOP button was pressed immediately when the vibrations had died away and the 
frequency was recorded. The experiment was repeated for the value of string lengths of 50, 60, 
70, and 80cm. 
Finally a graph of frequency against   ⁄ was plotted indicating the slope of the graph.

DISCUSSION 
From the experiment, it was observed that the frequency was directly proportional to the inverse 
of the length of the wire. This can be seen from the table of results.  The smallest length of 40cm 
yielded the highest frequency of 0.124 kHz. The greatest length of 80 cm likewise yielded the 
smallest frequency of 0.061 kHz. The slope was determined from the graph as 50 Hz.m. The 
slope was used to find the mass per unit length of the wire which was found to be 1x10-3kgm-1. 
This mass per unit length was then used to find the density of the wire by dividing it by the 
cross-sectional area of the wire. Density of the string was found to be 14147.11kg/m3. The 
literature density of nickel is 8900 kg/m3. This indicates a significant difference in the values. 
This great difference may be due to distortion in the shape or bends in the string leading to a 
change in volume. This affected the accuracy of the experiment. 
 
SOURCES OF ERROR 
1. The frequencies were hardly stable hence making readings difficult  
2. Error due to the non uniformity of the string hence affecting the mass per unit length

PRECAUTIONS 
1. It was ensured that the tensioning force was not set beyond 10N in order to prevent the 
string from breaking 
2. The experiment was repeated and the average frequency found to ensure accurate results. 
3. It was ensured that throughout the experiments the tension was kept constant 
 
CONCLUSION 
The density of the nickel wire calculated (14147.11kgm-3) is not similar to the theoretical value 
of 8900kgm-3 meaning that the experiment was faulty.
