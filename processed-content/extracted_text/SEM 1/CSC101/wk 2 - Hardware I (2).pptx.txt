--- SLIDE 4 ---
Data Representation
In order to understand how computers process data, it is essential to understand how they represent data.
Data can be represented in analog or digital form
Analog is continuous
E.g. 1.0, 1.01, 1.02, etc
Digital is discrete
E.g. 1, 2, 3, 
Binary Digits (bit)
2 states
On / True / Electric Charge Present 


Off / False / Electric Charge Absent

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Control Unit
Directs and coordinates computer operations
Interprets instructions
Initiates appropriate action to carry out the instruction
Controls and directs the ALU, registers and buses

--- SLIDE 11 ---
ALU
Performs arithmetic and comparison operations
Arithmetic – addition, division, multiplication & subtraction
Comparison – less than, greater than, equal

--- SLIDE 12 ---
Register
A staging area built into the CPU
Internal registers can only be accessed by the CPU
Some are user-accessible
Common types of registers
Instruction Register (IR)
Holds instruction that is currently being executed
Memory Buffer Register (MBR)
Holds data being transferred to/from main memory
Memory Address Register (MAR)
Holds the address (location) of the main memory for storing or retrieving data
Program Counter (PC)
Holds the address of the next instruction to be executed

--- SLIDE 15 ---
Bus Channel
A pathway for transmitting electrical signals between devices and components.
Bits travel on a bus
Data Bus
Transmit bits that represents actual data
Address Bus
Transmits bits that represent the location (address) of where the data resides in memory
Bus Width
Determines the number of bits that can be transmitted at one time
Bus width influences the processor’s word
Buses have clocks that controls transmission speed of the bus

--- SLIDE 16 ---
Bus contd.
Kinds of Buses
System bus
Connects the processor to the main memory
Part of the motherboard
Also called Front Side Bus (FSB)
Backside bus (BSB)
Connects the processor to the cache
Expansion bus
Connects the processor to other peripheral devices
E.g. video card
Examples of buses
USB (Universal Serial Bus)
SATA 
SCSI (Small Computer System Interface)
PCI
Thunderbolt

Power Supply
Computers run on electrical power
Electrical charge
Represents data (bits)
Provides power to drive the components
2 forms of electrical charge
Alternating Current (AC)
Unstable and spike prone
110 ~ 240 volts
Direct Current (DC)
Stable
5 ~ 15 volts
Power supply converts AC to DC
Contain a voltage switch
Fan to dissipate heat
