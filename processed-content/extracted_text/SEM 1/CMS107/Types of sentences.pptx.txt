What is a Sentence?
A group of words which expresses a complete thought. 
 Completeness of a sentence means the following: 
There should be subject and predicate.
There should be agreement between the subject and predicate. 
When written, the sentence should begin with a capital letter and end with an end mark such as the full-stop (period), question mark, or exclamation mark.

--- SLIDE 5 ---
Simple SENTENCES 
 A simple sentence is the one with one independent clause.  It contains only one idea, has a single clause with no dependent clause. It requires a subject and a verb.
The players arrived.
Aba danced beautifully.
Jesus wept.
Martin is a rich man.
James throws the ball.
James looks well.
James takes his bath.



--- SLIDE 6 ---
Compound SENTENCES 
COMPOUND SENTENCE. It is a sentence which consists of two independent clauses connected by a coordinating conjunction or when the two independent clauses are separated by a semi-colon.
Example
I apologized to her, but she did not forgive me.
Diana sings and Esi plays the piano.
Adu ran fast but he missed the train.


--- SLIDE 7 ---
COORDINATION
The process of joining two independent sentences into one sentence by using a coordinator is called Coordination. In coordination, the status of the sentences is not reduced when they are combined. In other words, when they are combined, they still remain independent clauses.
Meaning that a compound sentence is formed when two or more simple sentences are joined or coordinated together.  
Coordination, therefore, consists of constituents of equal elements. 

--- SLIDE 8 ---
COORDINATION
In English, we can have up to seven (7) coordinating conjunctions; for, and, nor, but, or, yet, and so. These coordinators are always placed between the clauses they coordinate. (FANBOYS-For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet and So).
The player performs well, for he trains regularly.
We went to the movies, and we ate potatoes.
He did not study for the exam, nor did he pass.
Visiting tourist sites can be very fulfilling, but it can be expensive.
NB: Remember to bring a comma before the coordinator.

--- SLIDE 11 ---
Subordination
Subordination is the process of joining or combining two or more dependent clauses to one or more independent clause(s) in a sentence structure. 
. Examples of subordinators include because, since, as, though, when, if, until, since, as soon as, as if, even if, unless, although, even though, hence, therefore, henceforth, because  etc.


--- SLIDE 12 ---
THE COMPLEX SENTENCE 
A complex sentence is formed when one main (independent) clause, and at least one subordinate (dependent) clause are joined by a subordinating conjunction(Quirk, 1973). 
Meaning that a complex sentence consists of one main clause, and one or more subordinate clauses subordinated to it.

Compound-complex SENTENCES 
COMPOUND-COMPLEX sentence  is a sentence which contains two independent clauses and one subordinate clause. 
A compound-complex sentence has at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. 
The compound-complex sentence combines elements of compound and complex sentences. 
Even though she was tired, Abby knew she had to finish the race so she ran to meet her team.
Although Ransford was late for the lecture, he organised himself within a few minutes and did a fantastic presentation.

TRIAL QUESTIONS
The University central committee reaches a decision today. The Students’ Representative Council will not support such a confrontational attitude.  (Coordination) 
I just graded her latest paper. She is capable of good work. She needs to be motivated. (Coordination) 
The students felt so relieved. The rains finally stopped. (Subordination)
I have visited you four times. You have visited me once. (Subordination)
Several graduates do not know what to do after graduating. The Universities need guidance experts to help them out. (Subordination).
There are too many hawkers on this campus. They leave the campus littered with garbage. They pose a health hazard. (Coordination and Subordination)
The messenger apologized for his sloppiness again. The supervisors paid no attention. They remembered the last time he rendered an apology for sloppiness. (Coordination and Subordination)
