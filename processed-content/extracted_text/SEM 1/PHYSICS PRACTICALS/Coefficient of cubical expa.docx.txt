EXPERIMENT 10
THE COEFFICIENT OF CUBICAL EXPANSION OF A LIQUID USING SPECIFIC GRAVITY BOTTLE
AIM
The aim is to determine the coefficient of cubical expansion of glycerine using the specific gravity bottle.
APPARATUS
Specific gravity bottle with a well- fitting stopper
Glycerine
Water-bath
Clamp and stand
Cotton thread 
Thermometer
Filter paper

THEORY
The coefficient of cubical expansion of a solid is defined as the change in volume per unit initial volume per unit change in temperature. It can also be defined as the fraction of its original volume by which the solid expands per degree rise in temperature.

PROCEDURE
The specific gravity bottle was cleaned with methylated spirit. It was allowed to dry in air with a fan over it. This empty bottle was weighed with its stopper on it. The temperature of glycerine in a beaker was determined using the thermometer and was poured into the bottle to its brim. The stopper was inserted which caused some of the glycerine to spill over. The glycerine was wiped off the bottle with a handkerchief and weighed. A cotton thread was tied around the neck of the bottle and the bottle was suspended from a clamp so that it was almost completely immersed in a water bath. The water bath was heated until the water started to boil for some minutes. The temperature of the water in the bath was recorded. The bottle was then removed, wiped it again, allowed to cool and then reweighed.

DISCUSSION
The coefficient of cubical expansion of glycerine was found to be 5.5912x10-4 K-1 and this is much larger than the coefficient of cubical expansion of glass of which the bottle is made. This implies that when the bottle was heated in the water bath, the glycerine expanded at a faster rate than the glass could and so some of the glycerine spilled over the brim of the bottle.
SOURCES OF ERROR
The temperature of the glycerine might have changed between the time it was taken and the time the glycerine was poured into the bottle.  
When the weight of the empty bottle was taken, the bottle was not completely dry due to the difficulty of cleaning glycerine which is slightly oily.
PRECAUTIONS
I handled the specific gravity bottle as little as possible to avoid warming it.
I removed the bottle out of the hot water with a napkin to avoid burning my fingers
CONCLUSION
The coefficient of cubical expansion of glycerine is 5.5912x10-4K-1.
