TITLE  :EXPERIMENT ON THE WATER EQUIVALENT OF A CALORIMETER.
AIM: To determine the water equivalent of a calorimeter.
APPARATUS
Copper calorimeter with outer jacket, stirrer made from bare copper wire, thermometer, beaker and a stove.
DIAGRAM
THEORY
Heat is a form of energy that a body possesses when heat flows into it. Heat energy can be measured in the units joule (J). Substances differ markedly in temperature rise caused by the absorption of a given amount of energy.   In general, when the temperature of a body changes by an amount Δt, the heat ΔQ that flows in or out of it is given by ΔQ = mcΔt where m is its mass and c is specific heat. The product mc is called the THERMAL CAPACITY or WATER EQUIVALENT of the body. Therefore, water equivalent of calorimeter is the measure of the quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of the calorimeter or make it one degree warmer. Water Equivalence is not the same for substances made from the same material because it depends on the mass of the material use for the substance. Therefore, water equivalence can also be defined as the number of grams of water, which  require the same amount of heat to cause a given change in temperature that is required to reproduce an equal change in the temperature of the inner vessel with the stirrer and thermometer.

METHOD
The calorimeter and stirrer were initially weighed together. Ice-cold water was then poured into the calorimeter until it was about one-third full, weighed, and placed in its outer jacket and left to stand until its temperature was about 5℃ below the room temperature. Meanwhile, some water was heated up in a beaker until its temperature was about 15℃ above the room temperature. The stove was removed from underneath the beaker and after stirring both the warm water and the water in the calorimeter; their temperatures were taken separately and as accurately as possible. Some of the warm water was then poured from the beaker into the calorimeter until it was about two-thirds full. It was stirred and the highest temperature reached by the mixture about 5℃ above room temperature was recorded. The calorimeter with its contents and the stirrer was weighed again.

DISCUSSION
From the experiment, the water equivalent obtained for the calorimeter was found to be 2.5g. In theory the water equivalent is 5.49g. The actual value was not obtained due to some errors during the experiment such as some impurities in the water, and the inconsistency in the room temperature and some personal errors such as overheating the water in the beaker which all affected the results.
SOURCES OF ERROR
Error may be to the use of impure water.
Error may be due to heat transfer from hand to the compartment while holding the stirrer to stir.
Error from slight spillage during the stirring process.
PRECAUTION
It was ensured that the mixture of warm water and the cold water in the calorimeter was well stirred to ensure uniform distribution of heat before the temperature was read.
It was ensured that the calorimeter was lagged (put in the outer jacket) to minimize the temperature loss in the water.
It was ensured that all temperatures were read accurately to obtain accurate results.
CONCLUSION
From the experiment, the water equivalent of the calorimeter is 2.5g.
