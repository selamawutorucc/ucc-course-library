TITLE: MEASUREMENT OF LOW RESISTANCE
AIM: To determine the resistances of some connecting cords of different lengths.
APPARATUS: ammeter, voltmeter, power source, amplifier, connecting cords, connection box, connecting cords of different lengths.
DIAGRAM
THEORY
The resistance of a metal conductor is the ability of the connecting cord to inhibit the flow of current passing through it. The resistance in connecting cords is due to collisions between electrons carrying current with fixed atoms within the cord. These collisions prevent the free flow of charges and hence prevent the flow of current. A connecting cord with low resistance allows large amount of current to pass through it.  A connecting with high resistance will only allow small amount of current to pass through it.
From Ohm’s law which states that when a voltage (potential difference) ∆V is applied across the ends of a conductor, the current in the conductor is found to be proportional to the applied voltage. This law will hold if the temperature of the conductor is kept constant.

The resistance is directly proportional to the length and inversely proportional to the area.
Where R is the resistance of the connecting cord, ρ is its resistivity, l is the length of the cord and A is the cross-sectional area of the cord. Since the resistance of a conductor is directly proportional to the length, an increase in length of the connecting cord means an increase in resistance in the cord. A decrease in the length of the cord will cause a decrease in resistance and hence allow more current to pass through.
METHOD:
A connection box, a power supply and an ammeter are connected in series by connecting cords. The input of the amplifier is connected of the sockets of the connecting cord of unknown resistance. The output of the amplifier is connected to the voltmeter. The voltage source is set to 12V. The current is varied at constant intervals of 0.2 A. The voltage drop across the connecting cord for each current selected is measured using the voltmeter. The experiment is repeated for the remaining cords of different lengths provided.
