Procedure
The experiment was set up as is shown in the diagram above. The viscometer was filled with the liquid glycerin. A temperature of 350C was maintained through the experiment using the thermostat and bath for about 15minutes. The alloy balls given were weighed on the electronic balance to determine their masses. The diameter of the ball was taken thereafter with the use of a micrometer screw gauge. Readings were obtained on each of the three ball bearings. These readings were recorded as d/mm. the ball bearings were dropped down the internal cylinder of the viscometer that is through the glycerin, one after the other. For each drop, the time taken for the ball to cover a specified distance was recorded using a stopwatch 
METHOD : The experimental set up was arranged in accordance with the figure above. The viscometer was adjusted with the adjustable feet on the stand base and the built-in spirit level. When swivelling the measurement section, the locking pin must be engaged in the slot inserted on the measurement section. The clean dry viscometer tube was filled with the liquid to be investigate , to just below the brim and the, and then the ball was inserted. Care was taken that there was no air bubbles in the liquid or adhering to the ball. It was found that the smallest bubbles detached themselves from the ball if it was pressed into the liquid with a glass rod. Then the hollow stopper was carefully inserted in the viscometer tube and secured. Before the measurements for temperature-dependence, the temperature was kept constant for 10 minutes. After the ball was placed in the gravity tube, viscometer was allowed to equilibrate to the selected working temperature T for approximately 10 minutes before 3-5 falling times t were determined.

Discussion
Viscosity refers to the degree of internal friction in a fluid. A highly viscous fluid is the one which possess high resistance on the motion of a body moving through it. Consider the equation proposed by Strokes, that is;
r2 = (η/ (σg - ρg)) Vt
Viscosity of a liquid is directly proportional to the square of the radius but inversely proportional to the velocity of the sphere through it.
 From the results obtained, it was observed that for the initial three stages of readings taking, the radius of the ball bearing decreased with an increase in velocity. It can be deduced that the decrease in the size of the ball bearings caused a decrease in the viscous drag of glycerine on the ball bearings and hence moved more and more smoothly with increasing velocities. 
Sources of error
-Error due to impurities in the glycerine 
-Error due to air bubbles in the glycerine.
-Error due to imperfect streamline due to dents or scratches on the ball bearing. 
Precaution
-It was ensured that the air bubbles were removed as much as possible.
-It was ensured that the internal cylinder in the viscometer was properly cleaned to avoid any other liquid from hindering the experiment and to produce a clear visible cylinder.
-Errors due to parallax were avoided during the reading of the distance on the cylinder.
Conclusion
From the results obtained from the calculation it can be concluded that for any spherical body moving down a liquid, as the size (square of the radius) increases, the viscous drag i.e. viscosity of the liquid increase to decrease the velocity of the body. Hence the viscosity of a liquid depends on the shape and the velocity of the falling body. The terminal velocity of the ball bearings falling through glycerin is small enough for it to be reached before turbulence occurs, and its measurement provides a method of determining the viscosity of such a liquid (glycerin).Viscosity also decreases with an increase in temperature.
