PROCEDURE:  
Firstly, the conical flask was filled to the brim (almost overflowing) with water.
The initial height was obtained by measuring the height of the water in the conical flask with the aid of a travelling microscope. The capillary tube was clamped on to the retort stand with about one quarter of it immersed in the water in the conical flask. The height to which the meniscus of the water rose is measured with the travelling microscope again.
The reading was taken from both the main and the vernier scale and recorded at once.
The capillary tube was removed from the water and then clamped in a horizontal position. The inner diameter of the tube was measured in order to get the radius of it. The entire process is repeated for two other tubes.

Sources of Error
Errors may be due to impurities in the water causing a reduction in the value of the surface tension.  
Errors may be due to fluctuations in temperature which did not give a stable surface tension value of water at a particular temperature.
The image of the diameter seen through the travelling microscope was not uniform hence the precise dimensions could not be obtained.
 Errors may be due to inaccurate calibration of the main and vernier scales of the slightly eroded travelling microscope.  
The mechanism of the travelling microscope that keeps the focus stationary was not functioning properly hence the level of the height at which the meniscus was kept on changing.
PRECAUTIONS
It was ensured that all measurements were repeated and their average found in order to avoid random error.
It was ensured that the height to which the meniscus rises in the capillary tube was measured at the eye level to avoid palarax.
It was ensured that the microscope was stable as readings were taken from the main and vernier scale.
It was ensured that the three capillary tubes were not interchanged as the experiment was being carried out.
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
 The theoretical value of the surface tension of water at 20 is 0.073N/m from College Physics. From the experiment however, the surface tension of water was found to be  0.417N/m at a temperature of 30. It can therefore be deduced that the surface tension of liquids decreases with increasing temperature. This can be attested to the fact that the faster moving molecules of a hot liquid are not bound together as strongly as are those in a cooler liquid. Also, the mechanism of the travelling microscope that keeps focus after the level of the meniscus has been located was not functioning properly this might have affected the readings of the meniscus resulting in errors. Therefore it was found that the height of the liquid kept on changing as readings were being taken. The diameter of the capillary tube observed through the microscope was not uniform so accurate values of the radii were not obtained. Lastly, the structure of the main scale as well as the vernier scale made it difficult for exact values to be read. This is because the whole scale could not be seen through the magnifying glass so one had to count the graduations and calculate the value. Moreover impurities in the water might have also contributed to the experimental error.
