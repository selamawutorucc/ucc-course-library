TITLE: The water equivalent of a calorimeter
AIM: To determine the water equivalence of a calorimeter using the principle of conservation of heat
APPARATUS: A copper calorimeter with an outer jacket, a stirrer made from a bare copper wire, a thermometer (calibrated from 0  to 100), a beaker, ice, electronic balance (Libror EB-3200s-A, serial number D400902191, Readability=0.1g), a clamp
DIAGRAM:
ELECTRONIC BALANCE
THEORY: 
Heat gained by calorimeter and cold water= Heat lost by warm water
MICW (3-1) + MCCC (3-1) = MFCW (2-3)
Where MI = Mass of cold water
              MC = Mass of calorimeter
              MF = Mass of warm water
              CW = Specific heat capacity of water
              CC = Specific heat capacity of calorimeter
.
                           MICW (3-1) + MCCC (3-1) = MFCW (2-3)
  But CC = CW,  since the water equivalent is the mass of water that has the specific capacity of the calorimeter.
                            Cw (MI+MC) (3-1) = MFCW (2-3)
                             (MI+MC)3-1) = MF (2-3)
But MI = M1 - M0,    MF = M2 - M1     and    Mc = w
(M1-M0+w) (3-1) = (M2-M1) (2-3)
PROCEDURE: The calorimeter and stirrer were first weighed together with the electronic balance and recorded as M0. Iced cold water then poured into the calorimeter to about one third its volume and was weighed with the stirrer and the mass was recorded as M1.  After the calorimeter was place in its outer jacket and left to stand until it acquires a temperature of about 5 below room temperature. Temperature is recorded as 1. Heated water of about 15 above room temperature, 2, was added to the water in the calorimeter increasing the volume to about two thirds the calorimeter’s. The mixture was then stirred with a stirrer and it maximum temperature reached was recorded with a thermometer, say 3. At this point the calorimeter and its content was weighed and recorded, say M2.
OBSERVATION:
CALCULATION: Heat gained by calorimeter and cold water = heat lost by warm water.
From the above,
(M1-M0+w) (3-1) = (M2-M1) (2-3) where
W is the water equivalent of calorimeter and stirrer.
(91.0-54.4+w) (31-22) = (127.6-91.0) (42-31)
W = - 36.6
     = 8.13g
PRECAUTION:
1. The thermometer was held with a clamp to avoid contact with the skin whose temperature might be recorded 
2. The calorimeter was well covered to ensure minimal loss of heat from the system.
3. The thermometer was wiped after ever reading to avoid introducing water clanged to it into new readings.
4. The thermometer was allowed to cool each time before taking readings to avoid transfer of heat which could affect the expected result.
CONCLUSION: The water equivalent of the calorimeter was found to be 8.13g
