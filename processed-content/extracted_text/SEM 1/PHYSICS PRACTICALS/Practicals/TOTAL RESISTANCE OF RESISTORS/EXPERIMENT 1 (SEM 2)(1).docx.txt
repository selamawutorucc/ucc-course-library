TITLE: WHEATSTONE BRIDGE EXPERIMENT
AIM: To determine unknown resistances, total resistance of resistors in series, and total resistance of resistors in parallel.
APPARATUS: Slide measuring bridge (1), connection box (1), standard PEK carbon resistor (1), unknown PEK carbon resistors (5), power supply, 5 V/1 A, +/-15 V (1), digital multimeter 071340.00 (1), three 500mm connecting cords (2 each; red, yellow and blue).
DIAGRAM:
THEORY: Wheatstone ridge is an instrument used in getting accurate resistance. This consists of four Resisters of resistance, R1, R2, R3 and R4 connected side by side together to form a closed circuit. The current flowing in the circuit divides into two branches. Varying the resistances, a stage is reached when no current flows through the galvanometer placed at the centre, indicating zero that is when C and Y are at the same potential (there is no potential between C and Y). The potential difference across R equals that across the length l2 and the potential difference across Rx equals l1.          Then R/RX =l2/l1   and Rx=R l1/l2
For resisters R1 and R2 in Series and Parallel the equivalent resistance are respectively
RX=R1 + R2 and 1/Rp =1/R1 +1/R2.
METHOD: 
In the experiment, the values of the resistors to be determined are made invisible and encoded as follows: A, B, C, and D. The resistance to be investigated (single, parallel-connected and series-connected) are as shown in the above diagram. Resistor A and the standard resistor 100Ω were connected using the connection box. Switching on the power supply, the correctness of the connection was checked by touching the bridge the bridge wire at the extreme ends (X and Y) with the slider jockey, J and the negative and positive readings of the digital multimeter G was observed. The position where there was zero reading on the multimeter represented the balanced point. The lengths l1 from the end X to the balanced point was measured.
The procedure was then repeated for the rest of the unknown resistances B (with a 680 Ω resistor), C and D. After that unknown resistors A and C were connected in series and then in parallel, and their values were recorded. Because the side wire measuring bridge gives the best reading accuracy in its central part, the measuring resistance R was brought to order of the magnitude of RX. The multimeter, G (setting 100mA = 100mV), was balanced by moving of the slider.

- Errors may have occurred because of delaying in opening the key on the connection box after readings were taken.
