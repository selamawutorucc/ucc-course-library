Course Code/Title: PHY 101 – GENERAL PHYSICS (2 credits) 
Course Contents: This course covers in an elementary manner the basic concepts of physics and its applications 
to real world problems.  
 
This course is intended to dispel the popular notion that physics is only accessible to smart people and also that 
physics is just a collection of equations used to solve obscure problems. Physics is interesting, useful, and 
valuable and it is fun and cool when you apply it to everyday life.  
It is one of the foundations on which other disciplines like astronomy, chemistry, geology etc. are built. Physics 
is the most fundamental science and it is concerned with the basic principles of the universe. 
General Physics I is the first course of a two semester introduction to general physics, preparing for careers in 
Biological and Physical sciences, science and mathematics education, Optometry, lab technology and Nursing.  
The main topics are Physical Quantities, Vectors, Kinematics, Work, Energy and Power, Fluids, and 
Thermodynamics.  
Our goal is for you to develop an understanding and intuition for physics so that you can solve practical 
problems. We feel that the only way to accomplish this goal is by thinking about and solving lots of problems 
and experimenting in the laboratory. 
We hope that Physics 101 will develop the critical thinking in collaborative skills you will need in your future 
career. Key to this is the need to help you gain conceptual understanding of the physical universe by studying 
the interrelationship between concepts that are the framework of physics and to apply mathematical tools as an 
aid in understanding those relationships. At the end of this course you would have improved your ability to 
reason and to think critically.  
You are welcome to our course. It is our hope that you will find this manual very useful and appropriate and by 
sticking to the guidelines in the course schedule it will help you to understand Physics concepts. 
 
PREREQUISITE:  
The prerequisites for this course include a) a strong desire to learn concepts in physics and b) a basic 
understanding of elementary mathematics and physics.

Subject content: 
1. Physical quantities, SI units, Derived units, conversions,  precision and accuracy 
2. Vectors, scalars, vector components, unit vectors, Graphical methods 
3. Dynamics, Force, mass, weight, Newton’s laws of motion, collisions, inertial frames of reference, 
friction 
4. Kinematics, Distance and displacement, speed, velocity, kinematic equations, circular, simple harmonic 
and projectile motions 
5. Elasticity, Stress, strain, modulus of elasticity 
6. Work, Energy & Power, Work done by a variable force, expression for kinetic energy, work-energy 
theorem 
7. Fluids, States of matter, hydrostatics, hydrodynamics 
8. Thermodynamics & Kinetic Theory, Atomic mass unit, Avogadro’s number, temperature scales, 
kinetic theory of gases, mean free path, heat energy, thermal conductivity, heat capacities, The 1st law of 
thermodynamics 
 
Course Learning Outcomes: 
By the end of this course, learners should be able to: 
 
1 Differentiate between SI units and other units 
2. Manipulate and appreciate vectors 
3. Distinguish between mass and weight 
4. Solve kinematic problems 
5. Know the states of matter
