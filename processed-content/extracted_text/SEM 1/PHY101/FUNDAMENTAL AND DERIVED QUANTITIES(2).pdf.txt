FUNDAMENTAL AND DERIVED QUANTITIES, STANDARD PREFIXES, UNIT 
CONVERSIONS   
 
In this chapter basic physical quantities used in the study of Physics are discussed. Standard units 
for the quantities mentioned are also given. Also, ways of writing very small or very large numbers 
are discussed. Writing such numbers in standard form helps you to avoid mistakes. Employing 
standard prefixes also simplifies your calculations. Measurement of length, time, temperature, 
mass and weight will be discussed. Instruments used for measuring each of these quantities are 
specified. And also dimension analysis is carried out to check the correctness of some of the 
equations used for calculations in Physics.

1.1 
Physical Quantities and Units 
Physics is sometimes defined as the study of energy and matter and their relationships. Physics is 
a natural science that also deals with measurement of physical quantities. We define a physical 
quantity either by specifying how it is measured or by stating how it is calculated from other 
measurements. For example, we define distance and time by specifying methods for measuring 
them, whereas we define average speed by stating that it is calculated as the ratio of distance 
traveled to the time taken. 
 
Measurements of physical quantities are expressed in terms of units, which are standardized 
values. For example, the length of a ladder, which is a physical quantity, can be expressed in units 
of meters. Without standardized units, it would be extremely difficult for scientists to express and 
compare measured values in a meaningful way. Thus, any number or set of numbers used for the 
quantitative description of a physical phenomenon is called a physical quantity.

Derived Quantities and Units 
Derived quantities are those that may be expressed in terms of base or derived quantities by means 
of the mathematical symbols of multiplication and division only (no addition or subtraction or any 
other sign). Basically they may be divided into two categories. The first group consists of derived 
quantities, which have proper units. Their units can be expressed in terms of units of base or 
derived units. The second class consists of quantities that do not have units expressed in terms of 
base or derived units.

1.4 
Measurement of Length  
Length is defined as the straight-line distance between two points along an object or in space. The 
SI unit of length is the meter. Different types of lengths are measured by using different types of 
instruments. Lengths like the length of cloth or length of a line can be measured by using measuring 
tape, a metre scale or a foot rule. But these instruments cannot be used to measure the diameter of 
a metal sphere or a cylinder. Micrometer screw gauge and Vernier calipers are the appropriate 
instruments used to measure the diameter of cylindrical objects.  
 
1.5 
Measurement of Time 
Time is defined as the period in which a process, action or an event takes place. Time is also called 
the Point or Period when something occurs. Time is a scalar quantity and its SI unit is the second 
(s). The instruments used for measuring time are stop clocks and stop watches. 
 
 
1.6 
Measurement of Temperature 
Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles of a substance. The higher 
the temperature of an object, the higher is its kinetic energy. Kinetic energy is a type of energy 
associated with motion. Temperature is measured with thermometers, which are devices that use 
the expansion of a substance to give an indirect measure of temperature. The units of temperature 
are called degrees Celsius (oC), degrees Fahrenheit (oF) or Kelvin (K). 
 
1.7 
Measurement of Mass and Weight 
Mass is a measurement of the quantity of matter contained in a body. The mass of an object is 
measured in kilogram (kg). Mass of objects can be measured by using a beam balance or an 
electronic balance.  
The weight of an object is proportional to the gravitational force of the earth on the object. 
Therefore Weight is the measurement of the pull of gravity on an object. Weight of an object can 
be determined with a spring balance. Weight of a body is directly proportional to it Mass. The 
more the mass of an object, the more weight it has.
