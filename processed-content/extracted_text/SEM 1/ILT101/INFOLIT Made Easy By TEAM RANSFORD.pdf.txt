SPECIFIC CRITERIA FOR COPYRIGHT PROTECTION 
 The work must be fixed in a definite medium of expression 
 The work must be original and the result of the author's / creator's skill and effort 
 The creator must be a citizen of the country or the work must have been published in the 
country before anywhere else. 
EXAMPLES OF PLAGIARISM ACTS 
 Copying a text from the internet, from a book or an article, without citing the sources and 
claiming ownership. 
 Rewriting a text in your own words without stating your source 
 Paying for an essay and presenting it as your own 
 Handing in another student’s work under your own name 
 Copying statistics, maps, tables or charts without stating your source. 
LEGAL EXCEPTIONS TO THE COPYRIGHT LAW 
 Authors or creators may transfer their monopolistic rights to publishers for marketing 
purposes. 
 Users / publishers have rights in the form of fair use. 
DEFINITION OF PLAGIARISM 
Plagiarism means claiming or implying original authorship of someone else's written or creative 
work, in whole or in part, into one's own without adequate acknowledgment.

DEFINITION OF CATALOGUING AND A CATALOGUE (DIFFERENCE BETWEEN 
THEM) 
 Cataloguing refers to the process of recording information about an item according to 
accepted standards. Such information includes author, title, edition, publisher, year, etc. 
 However, a library catalogue or a catalogue is a list of documents in a library, whereby 
the entries representing the documents are arranged in a systematic order. The library 
catalogue carries information on where the item can be found. 
WHAT THEN IS CLASSIFICATION: Classification is the process of grouping. It involves 
putting together like entities and unlike entities. 
THE TWO MAJOR SYSTEMS OF CLASSIFICATION 
 Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) System 
 Library of Congress Classification (LC) System 
EXCLUSIVE RIGHTS OF A CREATOR 
 The right to make copies 
 The right to be created for the work 
 The right to determine who may adapt the work to other derivative forms, such as 
translations, performance, financial benefit from the work and other related rights 
REASONS FOR CATALOGUING LIBRARY MATERIALS 
 To enable a library user find a book of which the title, the author or the subject is known. 
 To show what a library has by a given author on a given subject in a given kind of 
literature. 
 To assist library users in the choice of book as to its edition or as to its character.

SHORT NOTES ON THE FOLLOWING; 
SCHOOL LIBRARY: A school library refers to a library within a pre-tertiary institution. 
It is used by pupils / students and staff of a public or private school to have access to a 
variety of resources. It supports the various educational programs at the various levels. 
The collections of the school library may consist of printed works such as general 
reference materials and those that can be taken out like textbooks and novels. Non- 
printed materials may include films, audios and video recordings, computers, and CD-
ROMS. 
ACADEMIC LIBRARY: An academic library is also known to be a college or 
university library. Its users are the academic staff, researchers, students and 
administrative staff. It has a function of supporting teaching and learning and research 
activities of the institution. The adequacy of the academic library collections to support 
the curricula falls under the fundamental criteria to give accreditation for academic 
programmes. The collection reflects the academic programmes offered by the parent 
institution. These library resources include general reference books, textbooks, 
periodicals, newspapers, government publications in the print format and a wide range of 
electronic information. The main function of the academic library is for reference 
and reprographic services. 
SPECIAL LIBRARY: It is found in government institutions, research, commercial and 
business organizations to provide for information needs of their staff. It enables research 
workers and other employees to carry out their company duties effectively. Its collections 
include general reference materials, relevant books and periodicals in both printed and 
non-printed formats. Examples of special libraries include Supreme court of Ghana 
Library, Animal Research Institute Library, Food Search Library, Crops Research 
Institute Library, Oil Palm Research Institute Library. Special library employs Selective 
Dissemination of Information. 
FUNCTIONS OF A NATIONAL LIBRARY 
 Legal deposit 
 Compilation of national bibliography 
 Storage of numerous valuable works and rare books 
 Conducting research as a consultancy service for individuals and the nation 
 Practical training for library personnel

THE INTERNET 
SHORT NOTES ON THE FOLLOWING; 
INTERNET: The internet is a global network of computer networks. It is a large computer 
network that has connections to a vast number of computers all around the world that links 
millions of businesses, government agencies, educational institutions and individuals. The 
internet is not centrally controlled. Thus, owners of the various parts of the networks on the 
internet maintain them. 
BROWSERS: A browser refers to a software or program that is used to navigate or access the 
entire internet. Browsers can also be used for communication and retrieval of files on the internet. 
Without a browser on cannot access the internet. Text- based browsers and Graphic based 
browsers are the two types of browsers. Examples of graphic- based browsers are Netscape 
Navigator, Safari, Microsoft Internet Explorer. Examples of text-based browsers are Lynx and 
Mosaic 
BANDWIDTH: This refers to the speed and amount of information that is transferred from one 
place to the other on the internet. It is measured in bits per second (bps). The greater the 
bandwidth the greater the volume of information that could be transmitted at a time and the faster 
it will be to move information from one place to the other.

CRITERIA TO EVALUATE SEARCH 
 RELIABILITY: Information taken from the internet should be examined by checking 
the source if it could be relied upon. This is done by examining the Website's URL if the 
source is from an educational institution (.edu), a government agency (.gov), a non-profit 
organization (.org) or a commercial enterprise (.com), etc. 
 AUTHORITY: This is perhaps the major criterion used in evaluating information. 
Information are searched on the internet with some type of critical value, that is, the basis 
of authority with which the author speaks. Here are some possible factors considered;                                
                          The author's recognition in one’s field of study, reading of the author's 
biographical information to check his credentials, expertise on the topic and educational 
background if that author is not recognized. The author's position, institutional affiliation and 
address is also considered. 
 CURRENCY / TIMELINESS: This refers to the timeliness of the information. In 
printed documents, the date of publication is the first indicator of currency. On the 
internet, one can check the document date or the "last updated" date or date of copyright. 
 ACCURACY / COMPLETENESS: information for research purpose must be complete 
and objective with relevant supported facts. When evaluating information found on the 
internet, it is important to examine who is providing the information and the person's 
point of the view on the topic of research. 
 RELEVANCY: Relevancy refers to how well the information provided meets one's 
research needs. This is determined by considering how the information provided relates 
to the research topic. 
INTERNET SEARCH TOOLS AND THEIR MEANINGS 
 SEARCH ENGINES: A search engine is an information retrieval system designed to 
help find information found on a computer system (usually the World Wide Web). 
Search engines are made up of "spiders or robots " to collect information; to index 
this information; and create databases of websites visited which are regularly updated. 
They have search interface. Examples of SE includes www.google.com, 
www.ask.com, www.bing.com. A word or phrase called a search query can also be 
used in a search engine. 
 METASEARCH / MULTI-SEARCH ENGINES: Metasearch engines query 
several search engines at the same time and combine their results. They generate an 
ordered list based on comparative ranking. Metasearch engines permit users to enter

DEFINITION TO THE VARIOUS TERMS; 
 PHISHING: This is the act of stealing login information, often by sending emails 
that appear to come from a legitimate source such as a bank or a website like 
eBay. 
 COMPUTER VIRUS: It is a program or code that disrupts normal functioning of 
a computer system when loaded onto that computer 
 COMPUTER NETWORK: A computer network refers to a group of computers 
connected to each other so that they can share information and electronic 
resources. Computer network connections are either by cables, wire, telephone, 
satellite, etc. It comprises of two to over a million computers. One or more 
computers act as a host and the others are called clients. 
 SERVER OR HOST: This controls access to the resources on a network and also 
provides services and connections to other computers on the network. 
 DATABASE: This refers to a collection of structured records. It is structured in a 
way to allow orderly retrieval for research, study or administration. 
 ACADEMIC DATABASE: This is a universal index of periodic literature 
covering basic research from all fields of knowledge. They contain records with 
defined fields such as title, author, date, URL, etc. Examples include JSTOR, 
SAGE, Agora, etc. 
 INTERNET PROTOCOL: This refers to the set of rules that help the different 
computer networks on the internet to communicate without conflict. The 
protocols also determine whether the network uses a peer - to - peer or client / 
server architecture. The standard internet communication protocols are called 
Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and Internet Protocol (IP)
