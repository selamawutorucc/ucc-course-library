What is Computing?
• Computing refers to the use of computers, software, data, 
networks, algorithms, and related technologies to process 
information and solve problems.
• Computing includes:
• designing computer systems
• writing software
• storing and analysing data
• communicating through networks
• developing algorithms
• securing information
• automating tasks

What is a Computer?
• A computer is an electronic device that accepts data as input, 
processes the data according to instructions, stores data and 
information, and produces output.
• A basic computer system performs four major functions:
• Input → Processing → Output → Storage

--- PAGE 6 ---
Input–Process–Output–Storage Example
• Example: ATM transaction
• Input: Card, PIN, withdrawal amount
• Processing: Verify account and balance
• Output: Cash and receipt
• Storage: Transaction record

Computer Science
• Computer Science focuses on the principles and theories behind 
computation and the design of computational solutions.
• Major areas include:
• algorithms
• programming
• data structures
• artificial intelligence
• computer graphics 
• operating systems
• computer architecture
• theory of computation
• machine learning

--- PAGE 17 ---
Information Technology
• Information Technology focuses on the practical use, deployment, 
management, and support of computing technologies.
• Common areas include:
• networking
• system administration 
• cybersecurity
• cloud computing
• IT support
• infrastructure management 
• database administration

--- PAGE 18 ---
Information Systems
• Information Systems focuses on how organisations use 
people, processes, data, and technology to support 
operations and decision-making.
• Examples include:
• student information systems
• hospital management systems 
• banking systems
• payroll systems
• library management systems

Evolution of Computing
• Mechanical Calculators
• Pascaline - Developed by Blaise Pascal in the 17th

--- PAGE 25 ---
Generations of Computers
• First Generation
• Approximate period: 1940s–1950s.
• Main technology - vacuum tubes. 
• Characteristics:
• very large
• generated significant heat
• expensive
• consumed large amounts of electricity
• Example: ENIAC

--- PAGE 26 ---
Generations of Computers
• Second Generation
• Main technology - transistors. 
• Advantages over vacuum tubes:
• smaller
• faster
• more reliable
• consumed less energy
